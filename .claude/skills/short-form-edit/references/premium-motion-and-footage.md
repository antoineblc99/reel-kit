# Premium motion and readable footage

The first-three-seconds reason-to-stay gate remains the highest priority. This
craft pass improves its delivery; it does not replace curiosity with decoration.

For a minimal, Apple-style treatment, choose a coherent material family and one
focal action. Build readable object silhouettes, bevelled edges, thickness,
occlusion, directional lighting, and soft contact shadows. Use actual 3D when
camera rotation or assembly needs it. Keep typography clean and subordinate.
Avoid heavy text outlines, arbitrary glowing panels, fake chrome, uniform idle
bobbing, and repeated flat cards. Paper should have visible edges and perspective;
ceramic or metal should show form through light rather than noisy texture.

Inspect at phone size. Negative space should concentrate attention, not leave
the meaningful object too small. Compare adjacent shots without their headings.
Change action, scale, or camera only when it supplies new information. Alternate
assembly, detail, human expression, evidence, and a resolved wider view. Every
pattern interrupt needs an editorial purpose. Repeated B-roll is prohibited;
a callback uses a changed graphic state or a genuinely new source moment.

For each B-roll insert, save a ledger row with scene id, semantic sourceSceneId,
asset path relative to project, sha256, sourceStart, sourceEnd, and framing notes
for each delivery ratio. Source intervals refer to that exact asset file. Also
retain upstream provenance for excerpts. Detect duplicate scene IDs and overlapping
intervals from files with the same hash, including renamed copies. Re-encoding can
change hashes, so semantic scene identity and visual review remain necessary.

Use contain framing by default. An intentional crop must keep the complete
subject, visible action, and result inside a documented region throughout the
selection. Check start, midpoint, endpoint, and action peaks independently in
16:9 and 9:16. Do not enlarge a scene until essential context disappears or place
captions over the evidence. Phone-scale review determines whether a screen demo
communicates anything; passing dimensions alone is insufficient.

Normalize the voice first. If music was overwhelming, measure its reduction
against the previous stem and preserve the quieter bed-to-voice ratio through
mastering. Add varied SFX tied to physical motion and narrative reveals, not an
arbitrary cue-count quota. Check onset, contact peak, tail, and masking. State
whether a real listening pass was possible; RMS and ASR do not replace hearing.

Save concrete revision evidence. A requested 10/10 is a quality aspiration, not
a score an internal validator can award.
